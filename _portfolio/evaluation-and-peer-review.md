Language models are increasingly used as evaluators: they grade answers, assess student responses, and are proposed as aids for reviewing scientific papers. We study whether these judgments align with human ones, whether the rationale a model gives actually reflects what drove its decision, and how reliable evaluation is, both by models and by people.

We build large-scale resources for this, such as [LFQA-HP-1M](https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.425/), a human-preference dataset for long-form question answering, and [S-GRADES](https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.368/), which studies how student-response assessment generalizes across evaluative settings.

On the scholarly side, we look at how research is assessed: [the reproducibility of published work](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3627673.3679831), [generating and extracting the limitations of scientific papers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18207), and [predicting scholarly impact](https://aclanthology.org/2025.sdp-1.11/).

**Keywords:** alignment with human judgment · reasoning faithfulness · peer review reliability
