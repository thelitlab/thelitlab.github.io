A model that scores well on a benchmark may have learned the task, or a shortcut that happens to work on that benchmark. We study how models behave under domain transfer and adversarial perturbation, and use interpretability methods to find out what they have actually learned and why it does or does not carry over to new data.

Examples include [a multi-dimensional study of how pretrained models generalize on NLI and reading comprehension](https://aclanthology.org/2024.genbench-1.11/) (awarded at GenBench@EMNLP 2024), [when reading-comprehension models understand language and when they rely on shortcuts](https://aclanthology.org/2022.coling-1.8/), and how annotation artifacts affect what probing reveals ([CoNLL 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13856), [COLING 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07102.pdf)).

**Keywords:** domain transfer · adversarial perturbation · shortcut learning
